## 1. General Provisions

### 1.1 Purpose and Scope

This manual establishes the policies and procedures governing the administration of public assistance programs including the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP), Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF), and Medicaid. All caseworkers must follow these procedures when processing applications, conducting reviews, and making eligibility determinations.

### 1.2 Definitions

- **Applicant**: Any individual who has submitted an application for benefits under one or more programs covered by this manual.
- **Caseworker**: A county employee authorized to process applications, conduct reviews, and make eligibility recommendations.
- **Eligibility Determination**: The formal process of evaluating an applicant's qualifications against program-specific criteria.
- **Recertification**: The periodic review process required to continue receiving benefits.
- **Household**: All individuals living together who purchase and prepare food together (SNAP) or who are related and living together (TANF/Medicaid).

### 1.3 Caseworker Authority

Caseworkers are authorized to perform routine administrative actions including status updates, document requests, and scheduling. Caseworkers may NOT unilaterally terminate benefits, deny applications, change payment amounts, or issue legal notices without supervisory review and approval.

## 2. SNAP (Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program)

### 2.1 Eligibility Criteria

#### 2.1.1 Income Thresholds

SNAP eligibility is determined by gross monthly income relative to the Federal Poverty Level (FPL). The following gross monthly income limits apply:

| Household Size | Monthly Income Limit (130% FPL) | Annual Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $1,580 | $18,960 |
| 2 | $2,137 | $25,644 |
| 3 | $2,694 | $32,328 |
| 4 | $3,250 | $39,000 |
| 5 | $3,807 | $45,684 |

Households with gross income exceeding 130% FPL are ineligible for SNAP benefits unless they contain an elderly or disabled member, in which case the net income test at 100% FPL applies.

#### 2.1.2 Residency Requirements

Applicants must reside in the county and provide proof of residency. There is no minimum duration-of-residency requirement for SNAP.

#### 2.1.3 Required Documentation

All SNAP applications and recertifications must include:
- Government-issued photo identification
- Proof of income (pay stubs, employer letter, or tax return from the most recent filing year)
- Completed application or recertification form
- Proof of residency (utility bill, lease agreement, or official mail)

### 2.2 Recertification

#### 2.2.1 Recertification Timeline

SNAP benefits must be recertified every 12 months. Recertification notices are sent 60 days before the certification period expires. Applicants must submit all required documentation at least 15 days before the expiration date.

#### 2.2.2 Late Recertification

If an applicant fails to submit recertification documents by the deadline, benefits continue for a 30-day grace period. During this period, the caseworker must make at least two contact attempts (phone and mail). If no response is received within the grace period, benefits are suspended — not terminated — pending further contact.

### 2.3 Income Changes

#### 2.3.1 Reporting Requirements

Applicants must report income changes exceeding 10% of their current reported income within 10 days of the change. Failure to report may result in overpayment recovery.

#### 2.3.2 Income Change Processing

When an income change is reported or discovered, the caseworker must:
1. Verify the new income with documentation
2. Recalculate eligibility using the updated income
3. If the applicant remains eligible: update the benefit amount and notify the applicant
4. If the applicant becomes ineligible: initiate the eligibility redetermination process (see Section 2.4)

### 2.4 Eligibility Redetermination

#### 2.4.1 When Required

Eligibility redetermination is required when:
- Income exceeds program thresholds
- Household composition changes
- Residency status changes
- An applicant requests voluntary withdrawal

#### 2.4.2 Redetermination Process

The caseworker must:
1. Send written notice to the applicant citing the specific reason for redetermination
2. Allow 15 calendar days for the applicant to respond with updated documentation
3. Schedule an in-person or phone interview if documentation is unclear
4. Make a determination based on all available evidence
5. Issue a formal determination letter within 5 business days of the decision

**Important:** Benefit termination resulting from redetermination requires supervisory approval. The caseworker prepares the recommendation; the supervisor executes the termination.

## 3. TANF (Temporary Assistance for Needy Families)

### 3.1 Eligibility Criteria

#### 3.1.1 Income Thresholds

TANF eligibility requires gross monthly income below 50% FPL:

| Household Size | Monthly Income Limit (50% FPL) | Annual Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | $822 | $9,864 |
| 3 | $1,036 | $12,432 |
| 4 | $1,250 | $15,000 |
| 5 | $1,464 | $17,568 |

#### 3.1.2 Work Requirements

All non-exempt TANF recipients must participate in at least 30 hours per week of approved work activities. Approved activities include: employment, job search, vocational training, community service, and GED/education programs (limited to 12 months).

#### 3.1.3 Time Limits

TANF benefits are limited to a cumulative maximum of 60 months. When a recipient reaches 54 months, the caseworker must:
1. Send a written time-limit approaching notice
2. Schedule a transition planning meeting
3. Provide referrals to employment services and other community resources
4. Document the transition plan in the case file

### 3.2 Check-in Requirements

#### 3.2.1 Monthly Check-ins

TANF recipients must complete monthly check-ins documenting their work activities. Check-ins may be completed in-person, by phone, or through the online portal.

#### 3.2.2 Missed Check-ins

If a recipient misses a scheduled check-in:
- **First missed check-in:** Caseworker sends a reminder notice and reschedules within 5 business days.
- **Second consecutive missed check-in:** Caseworker initiates a compliance review. Benefits continue during the review.
- **Third consecutive missed check-in:** Benefits may be sanctioned (reduced by 25%) following supervisory approval. The recipient must be notified in writing with appeal rights.

### 3.3 Benefit Termination

#### 3.3.1 Grounds for Termination

TANF benefits may be terminated when:
- The 60-month time limit is reached
- Income exceeds eligibility thresholds
- The recipient fails to comply with work requirements after sanctioning
- The recipient voluntarily withdraws
- Fraud is substantiated

#### 3.3.2 Termination Process

**Benefit termination requires supervisory approval and must follow this process:**
1. Caseworker documents the basis for termination with supporting evidence
2. Caseworker prepares a termination recommendation memo
3. Supervisor reviews and approves or rejects the recommendation
4. If approved: a formal termination notice is issued to the recipient at least 15 days before the effective date
5. The notice must include: the reason for termination, the effective date, and the recipient's right to appeal
6. The recipient has 30 days from the notice date to file an appeal

## 4. Medicaid

### 4.1 Eligibility Criteria

#### 4.1.1 Income Thresholds

Medicaid eligibility is based on Modified Adjusted Gross Income (MAGI) relative to FPL:

| Category | Income Limit |
|---|---|
| Adults (19-64) | 138% FPL |
| Children (0-18) | 200% FPL |
| Pregnant individuals | 200% FPL |

#### 4.1.2 Residency Requirements

Applicants must be residents of the state and intend to remain. There is no minimum duration-of-residency requirement.

#### 4.1.3 Required Documentation

- Government-issued identification
- Proof of income (most recent tax return, pay stubs, or self-employment records)
- Proof of residency
- Social Security number or immigration documentation

### 4.2 Renewal Process

#### 4.2.1 Annual Renewal

Medicaid coverage must be renewed annually. The agency first attempts an ex-parte (automatic) renewal using available data sources. If eligibility cannot be confirmed automatically, a renewal form is sent to the enrollee.

#### 4.2.2 Renewal Timeline

- Renewal notices are sent 90 days before the coverage expiration date
- Enrollees have 30 days to respond
- If no response: a final reminder is sent with a 15-day deadline
- If still no response: coverage is terminated at the end of the coverage period

### 4.3 Coverage Continuity

#### 4.3.1 No Gaps Policy

To the extent possible, Medicaid coverage should not have gaps. If an enrollee's renewal is pending and they seek services, the presumption is continued coverage until a formal determination is made.

## 5. Appeals Process

### 5.1 Right to Appeal

Any applicant or recipient has the right to appeal any adverse decision, including denial, reduction, suspension, or termination of benefits. This right must be communicated in writing with every adverse action.

### 5.2 Appeal Timeline

- Appeals must be filed within 30 days of the adverse action notice
- The agency must schedule a hearing within 45 days of receiving the appeal
- Benefits continue during the appeal period if the appeal is filed before the effective date of the adverse action

### 5.3 Appeal Processing

The caseworker must:
1. Acknowledge receipt of the appeal within 3 business days
2. Compile the complete case file for the hearing officer
3. Not take any further adverse action on the case during the appeal period
4. Document the appeal and all related communications in the case timeline

## 6. Communication Standards

### 6.1 Official Notices

All official notices must:
- Be written in plain language at a 6th-grade reading level
- Include the applicant's name and case number
- Clearly state the action being taken and the reason
- Include the applicant's rights, including appeal rights where applicable
- Be sent via certified mail for adverse actions

### 6.2 Routine Communications

Routine communications (appointment reminders, document requests, status updates) may be sent via:
- Mail
- Phone
- Secure electronic portal
- In-person delivery

Caseworkers should document all communication attempts in the case timeline.

---

## 7. Data Security and Privacy

### 7.1 Confidentiality

All applicant and recipient information is confidential and protected under federal and state privacy laws. Caseworkers may only access case information for cases assigned to them or with supervisory authorization.

### 7.2 Record Retention

Case records must be retained for a minimum of 5 years after the case is closed. Electronic records must be backed up and stored in compliance with the agency's data retention policy.
